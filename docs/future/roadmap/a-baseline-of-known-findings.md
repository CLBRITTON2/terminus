A review can be told what is already known about a project, so that it reports what is new and a batch can be clean against the list.

## why

On mega, between 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19, six reviews of ordinary batches each confirmed the batch's own work and then blocked on five or six older findings in the files it touched. Every finding was real. Several had already been written up as roadmap cards, and came back on the next pass as if new, in slightly different words. The implementer was told to list an older finding once and leave it, and had to recognise by eye that a finding was the same one.

Two costs followed. A batch that was correct could not be shown clean, so "clean" stopped meaning what the practice needs it to mean. And nobody could ask the more useful question: beyond what is on the list, what else is there?

## shape to explore

- **A list of known findings per project,** each with an identity that survives rewording: the file, the quality, and what is claimed.
- **A review run against it** reports new findings as blocking and known ones as known, and is clean when nothing new blocks.
- **Each known finding points at where it is tracked,** so the list cannot become a place to hide things. An entry with nothing behind it is itself a finding.
- **A finding ruled not a fault** goes into the quality as something not to flag, which is what a canon already does. The baseline is for faults accepted for now, not for rulings.

## to settle

How a finding is recognised as the same one when a reviewer words it afresh each time. Whether a known finding in lines the change did touch should stop being excused. And how the list is kept from growing without anyone deciding.

Captured by the planning agent on mega.

## a caution from the same day

Michael's answer to the situation that prompted this card was the opposite one: review the full tree and work through every finding until it is clean, as every other project of his has done. A clean tree needs no baseline. So this is for a project that decides not to go to clean, and it is worth asking whether such a project should exist before building anything for it.
